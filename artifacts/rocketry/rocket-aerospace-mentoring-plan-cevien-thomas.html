Teaching approach

  • One concept, one build. Each Wednesday has a 30-minute idea and a hands-on activity that produces something: a sketch, a CAD part, a simulation, or data.

  • High school math only. Algebra, ratios, and F = ma. No calculus; graphs do the heavy lifting.

  • Real decisions, not just facts. Dr. Busari wants her to learn how engineers make design decisions, so every activity ends with "why did we choose this?"

  • Tie it to our rocket. Use the lab's LOX/RP-1 test rig as the running example: tanks, manifolds, valves, and why each exists.

  • She drives, you guide. Let her click, build, and make mistakes; you ask questions.

Safety and logistics

She's a minor in a lab with pressurized propellant hardware, so the rules are simple and non-negotiable.

Working with a minor

  • Work in the open lab with others around; avoid being alone with her in a closed room.

  • Communicate by school or university email only, copying Dr. Busari or Ms. Unoko. No personal texts or social media.

  • Ask Dr. Busari whether Morgan State requires minors-on-campus training or a background check for mentors. Many universities do; I couldn't find Morgan's policy online.

  • Get CNHS media consent before posting any photos of her.

In the lab

  • Safety glasses and closed-toe shoes every session.

  • Hands off the rig: no pressurized lines, LOX, RP-1, or helium cylinders.

  • No power tools or soldering unless you're right beside her.

Model rocket motors (per the NAR Model Rocket Safety Code)

  • Certified commercial motors only; electrical ignition with a safety interlock.

  • After a misfire, disconnect and wait 60 seconds before approaching.

  • At least 15 ft from D-class or smaller motors; outdoors only.

  • The code covers flights, not static tests, so Dr. Busari signs off the static test plan (Mar 3 gate).

Logistics

  • Track her hours for CNHS each week.

  • Sort out lunch on day 1; she's on campus 9 AM to 2 PM.

  • If you can't make a Wednesday, tell Dr. Busari and Ms. Unoko ahead of time.
