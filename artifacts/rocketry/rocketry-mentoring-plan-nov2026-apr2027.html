The approach: professional process, beginner-level content

Keep the work professional, but teach every idea at her level. "Professional" means how she works — notebook, requirements, simulation, design review, test plan, real data, technical presentation — not how advanced the math is. A high school student can do every one of those habits for real.

Every topic goes up the same three-step ladder. If she is lost, go back one step, never forward.

  1. See it — a demo, toy, video or real part in her hands (5–10 min).

  2. Do it — she builds, tests or clicks it herself (most of the session).

  3. Name it and number it — only now the correct term, and at most one simple equation.

Drop from her required learning: NASA CEA, O/F ratios, chamber temperature, LOX/RP-1 chemistry, Isp derivations. These become a short show-and-tell of what the university team does (Jan 27), not material she must master.

How to explain the hard ideas

Give the everyday picture first, the hands-on second, the correct term last.

ConceptEveryday pictureHands-on
ThrustA balloon zooming when you let goBalloon on a string
1 newtonAbout the weight of a small appleHold an apple, then a 100 g weight
Center of gravity (CG)The balance pointBalance the rocket on one finger
Center of pressure (CP)Where the wind pushes hardestWeathervane, or feathers at the back of an arrow
StabilityArrow (CG ahead of CP) flies straight; a dart thrown backwards tumblesSwing test on a string
DragHand out of a car windowFlat hand vs. edge-on hand in front of a fan
Total impulseHow hard × how long you push a swingCount squares under the thrust curve
Load cellA digital kitchen scalePress it, watch numbers change
CalibrationZeroing a scale and checking it with known weightsHang 100 g, 500 g, 1 kg

Questions that check understanding better than "Do you understand?":

  • "Show me where…"

  • "What would happen if we made the fins bigger?"

  • "Explain it to me like I'm your little cousin."
