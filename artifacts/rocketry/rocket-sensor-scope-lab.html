Try this with her

  1. Fire in Single mode. Count squares: how tall is the peak, and how wide is the burn?
  2. Turn volts/div down to 0.1 V and fire again. The peak runs off the top. Why?
  3. Drag the trigger level to 2 V and fire. Nothing is caught, because the signal never crosses it.
  4. Switch to Roll and fire. The trace scrolls like a heart monitor instead of freezing.
  5. Pick the pressure sensor. Before firing the line sits at 0.5 V, not 0 V. Ask her why.

How the signal travels

1

Something physical happens

The motor pushes on the stand with a force, measured in newtons.

2

The sensor turns it into voltage

The load cell's metal bends a tiny bit. Strain gauges glued to it change resistance, giving a few millivolts.

3

The signal is made bigger

Millivolts are too small to see, so an amplifier multiplies them by 1000 into volts.

4

The scope draws it

Up is volts, across is time. The trigger catches the moment the firing starts.

5

Back to real units

Divide the volts by the calibration number to get newtons again.

On our thrust stand: the HX711 board is a digital converter. It sends numbers to the Arduino instead of a smooth voltage, so the Arduino records the thrust curve rather than the scope. The picture on the scope here is what an analog amplifier would show, and it is the same curve you will plot in Google Sheets. Pressure-sensor numbers on this page are illustrative, not data from our rig.
