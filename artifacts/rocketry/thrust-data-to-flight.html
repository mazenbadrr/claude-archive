Thrust Data to Flight

A thrust curve is not the end of the test. Engineers use it to answer three questions before anything flies: how much push the motor gives, whether the rocket can lift off, and how high it goes before the parachute should open.

One test, three answers

Question 1

How much total push does the motor give?

Push hard for a short time or gently for a long time: what counts is how hard × how long. On the graph that is the area under the curve. Each grid square is 2 N × 0.2 s = 0.4 N·s, so counting squares gives the total impulse.

Question 3

How high will it go, and when should the parachute open?

The motor burns for about 1.9 s, then the rocket coasts upward and slows down. The second number in C6-5 is a delay: 5 seconds after burnout a small charge pushes the parachute out. The best delay opens it right at the top, when the rocket is barely moving.

Where else engineers use this data

Simulation software

A thrust curve can be saved as a motor file (.eng). OpenRocket then flies her own rocket design with her measured motor instead of the catalog one.

Quality check

Compare the measured curve with the certified one. A big difference means a bad motor, a bad stand, or a calibration mistake, and the test plan should say how to tell which.

Strength of parts

The 14 N peak is about the weight of a 1.4 kg bag. The motor mount and its glue must hold that push every flight.

Big engines

On liquid engines, live pressure and thrust data tell the team whether the engine is healthy, and are used to shut it down automatically if a number goes out of range.

Ask her: the Estes product page lists 10 N·s and 15.3 N for the C6-5, but the NAR certification test gives 8.8 N·s and 14.1 N. Which would she trust for her report, and why? Her own test is the tie-breaker.
