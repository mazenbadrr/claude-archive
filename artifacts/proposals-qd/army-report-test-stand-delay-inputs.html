Purpose: To explain the delay to the Lake Clifton test campaign, as input to the Army status update

1. Executive summary

The first Lake Clifton hot-fire is now scheduled for 29 Mar – 9 Apr 2027, after a cold-flow window of 9–25 Mar 2027. The delay is not caused by hardware: the test stand has been fabricated and tack-welded at McKinney Corp. The delay comes from the University's requirement that a licensed Professional Engineer certify every part of the facility before we build or test on University land.

That requirement turned into three separate engineering procurements, each run by MSU Procurement under Maryland procurement law:

  1. Structural PE — seal the concrete foundation and anchorage (PWS-LC-FND-001, started July 2026).

  2. Fire protection PE — independent hazard, quantity-distance and code review for LOX/RP-1 (PWS-LC-FPE-001, submitted 11 Sep 2026).

  3. Test stand PE — review the steel stand design and sign off on its assembly (through McKinney, raised 30 Oct 2026).

Each one went through repeated revisions. Procurement changed the contracting method, asked for legal and liability language to be rewritten, and asked for University policy text to be quoted word for word. Site readiness added more time: demolition at Lake Clifton continues through 2026, and permanent electrical service is expected in early 2027.

4. McKinney Corp: test stand fabrication

McKinney Corp is fabricating the steel test stand, and the stand is now physically built and tack-welded. McKinney was chosen because our first quote (Metal Supermarkets, 1 Jul 2026, $10,734.69 for waterjet-cut plates) could not cover the corner welds. We needed a shop that could cut, weld, assemble and finish in one place.

Status as of the 1 Nov 2026 shop visit:

  • Tubes cut, structure bolted together following about 95% of our assembly sequence, then checked for squareness, parallelism and flatness before tack welding. The technician will finish the final welding.

  • McKinney can also powder-coat the stand, roll the fin can and covers, make carbon-fiber tubes and the nose cone, machine the manifolds, and clean parts ultrasonically for LOX service.

  • One issue was recorded. Parts we had put on hold for design changes were waterjet-cut anyway. McKinney asked us to send the final changes, and the revisions are going ahead.

Figure 2. Tack welding after the squareness, parallelism and flatness checks passed (Oct 2026).

How McKinney can help with certification: on 30 Oct 2026 we proposed that McKinney assemble the stand in their shop, ship it, and reassemble it on site. Murph (McKinney) offered to find a PE who will review the full stand design against our expected loads, sign off on the assembly, and take responsibility for the whole stand. A cost estimate is coming. The open question is whether the University will accept a PE licensed outside Maryland. The foundation PWS requires a Maryland license at the time of sealing, which can be obtained through comity (a state accepting another state's license).

5. Structural PE certification

The concrete foundation and anchorage can't be poured until a Maryland-licensed structural PE verifies, seals and certifies the design. The team's engineering is finished. What remains is hiring and contracting that PE, and that process has taken four months so far.

What the PE certifies: a reinforced-concrete pad with 16 Hilti HDA-TR M12 stainless undercut anchors. In our analysis, overturning governs at a factor of safety of 3.52 (1.76 with a dynamic amplification factor of 2.0). The PE also covers drainage and spill containment: a level deck, a sloped apron, a trench drain and a buried double-wall tank of about 300 gal.

6. Fire protection PE certification

No propellant can be stored or fired on site until a Maryland-licensed fire protection engineer independently reviews the design and issues signed and sealed documents. The Baltimore City Fire Marshal must also approve the site. As of 3 Nov 2026, Procurement has picked EBL Engineers and asked for an itemized quote. EBL had not yet confirmed receipt.

What the engineer reviews:

  • 38.5 lbm RP-1 and 99.4 lbm LOX on the stand for each firing; ready storage of 1,157 lbm LOX and 370 lbm RP-1.

  • Firings: about 5 s to verify ignition and 12.4 s to depletion, priced on a 30 s envelope.

  • Applicable codes: the IFC 2021 hazardous-materials chapters, NFPA 55 and 30, quantity-distance siting, and NFPA 15 water spray.

  • Eight deliverables (D1–D8): site assessment, code and hazard analysis, fire protection design, emergency plan, agency coordination, and sealed documents with acceptance records.

Why it took this long: the team submitted a complete package on 11 Sep 2026 (a 47-page PWS and a 69-page Quantity-Distance Analysis) and got no response for four weeks. The first RFP draft came back on 12 Oct with the old scope values and text copied from an IT/software template, such as an "Enterprise Ticketing Platform" and a SOC 2 audit. On 20 Oct, Procurement dropped the RFP and chose to piggyback on an existing school-district contract with EBL. EBL's experience is mainly in school buildings, so Dr. Busari made the award conditional on EBL meeting the PWS qualifications, or adding a specialty subconsultant who does.

8. Path to first hot-fire

The March–April 2027 windows hold only if all three PE contracts are signed soon. Each review, the pad construction and the Fire Marshal approval all have to finish before cold-flow begins on 9 Mar 2027.

Construction can't start without the structural sign-offs, and no propellant can come on site without the fire protection engineer's sealed documents.

Actions needed to hold the schedule:

  • Procurement issues the structural PWS Rev. 3 solicitation.

  • EBL submits its itemized quote, shows LOX/RP-1 qualifications (or adds a specialty subconsultant), and a contract is signed.

  • The University decides whether it accepts an out-of-state PE for the stand. The team sends the stand loads to McKinney.

  • Facilities confirms the date for permanent site power.
