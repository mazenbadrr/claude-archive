Overview

Five simulations cover the demo rig: CFD gives the thrust and torque of Prop A vs Prop B, a second CFD sizes the tank-wall error, and FEA, modal and FSI checks confirm the printed parts are strong and stiff enough not to distort the measurement. The BEMT and Monte-Carlo runs are already done and give the blind prediction.

CFD of the thruster (Ansys Fluent)

A steady MRF bollard simulation of each propeller at three speeds gives the torque difference directly; Prop A is modelled smooth with transition, Prop B fully turbulent with sand-grain roughness from its measured Ra.

CFD of tote confinement

One extra run puts the thruster inside the tote to measure how much the walls and recirculating jet change thrust; the factor corrects the measured absolute values, while the A-vs-B ratio needs no correction.

  • Domain: the tote interior, 800 × 500 mm, water depth 330 mm; thruster axis 160 mm above the floor, 250 mm from the back wall, jet toward the far end.

  • Free surface: rigid lid (symmetry plane). Valid while the surface stays flat; check the pressure under the lid stays below 2 % of the jet dynamic pressure.

  • Same thruster mesh and rotating zone as section 2, Prop A at 1500 rpm.

  • Output: confinement factor = KT(tote) / KT(open water), and the same for KQ. Expect a few percent; above 10 % means use a bigger tank for the paper tests.

FEA of printed parts

Hand checks show every load-path part has a safety factor of 4 or more at the demo load (thrust 15 N, torque 0.12 N·m). The lowest margin is the motor-mount struts at full-rig load, so print them solid. Ansys Mechanical should confirm these numbers and show the deflections.

Ansys Mechanical setup:

  • Materials (printed, conservative): PETG E = 1.9 GPa, ν = 0.38; PLA E = 3.0 GPa; standard SLA resin E = 2.2 GPa, reduce strength 20 % for water uptake; PVC E = 2.9 GPa. Use the across-layer strength as the allowable wherever stress runs across layers.

  • Boundary conditions: revolute joint at the M8 pivot; compression-only support at the finger tip (the scale); fixed supports at the pillow-block feet.

  • Loads: duct thrust (57 % of total) as a remote force on the duct inner surface; rotor thrust (43 %) and torque on the motor-mount plate; gravity; buoyancy ignored (conservative).

  • Mesh: quadratic tetrahedra, 1 mm, refined to 0.3 mm at the inserts and strut roots; confirm stress changes < 5 % on refinement.

  • Load levels: demo (15 N, 0.12 N·m) and full rig (25 N, 0.2 N·m).

Modal and blade-deflection checks

The blade's first bending mode in water is estimated near 130 Hz, which falls inside the 6/rev strut-wake band at the demo speeds, so a wet modal run is worth doing before testing.

Hand estimate (cantilever blade, PLA): about 480 Hz dry; added water mass lowers it to roughly 130 Hz. This is a low-confidence estimate.

  • Wet modal (Ansys Mechanical): blade fixed at the hub; surround the propeller with an acoustic body (water, 1000 kg/m³, c = 1480 m/s) to include added mass. Plot the first 3 modes against rpm (Campbell diagram) for PLA and resin. If a mode lies within ±10 % of an excitation line at a test speed, move that test speed.

  • One-way FSI: map the Fluent blade pressure at 1750 rpm onto the blade in Mechanical (static). Accept tip deflection below 0.5 mm and pitch change at 0.7R below 0.3°. If either is exceeded, re-run the CFD on the deformed blade and report both.

Run order and acceptance criteria

The simulations are run in the order below, and each result is judged against the BEMT prediction and then the demo measurement.

  1. Static FEA of the rig parts (before printing): all safety factors ≥ 2 at full-rig load.

  2. Thruster CFD mesh study at 1500 rpm: grid convergence index on KQ below 3 %.

  3. Thruster CFD, Prop A and Prop B at 1250, 1500 and 1750 rpm.

  4. Wet modal and one-way FSI of the blade, using the CFD pressures.

  5. Tote confinement CFD: one run at 1500 rpm.

  6. Run the demo, then compare.
