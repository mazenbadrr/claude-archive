This is the design as it stands after the 29 Sep 2026 anchor decision (PWS-LC-FND-001 Rev. 0A). It combines the foundation package, the level-pad drainage revision and the Hilti HDA anchor change on one sheet. It is a working design for PE review. It is not sealed.

Plan

Sheet 1 · to scale

Four legs sit on a 63 in square centred on the 96 in pad. Each baseplate takes four anchors on a 4 in square, so the nearest anchor is 14.5 in from the pad edge. The 18 in apron on the side away from the operators slopes ½ in per foot. Water and any spilled RP-1 run down it into the trench, and the trench outlet drops straight into a buried tank. The dashed outline shows the tank below grade. It sits under the apron and trench, not under the pad, so the pad still bears on solid ground.

Section A–A

Sheet 2 · to scale · through legs at x = 16½ in

The pad, apron and trench encasement are poured as one monolithic block on 4–6 in of compacted stone, with the excavation 21 in below the pad top. Both rebar mats carry into the apron. The baseplates bear directly on the concrete, with no grout and no leveling nuts. The anchors are drilled after the pour, through the plates. The trench outlet drops vertically into the buried tank. The tank sits in clean backfill on a hold-down slab, and its top is 15 in below the pad soffit.

The drainage sheet (Aug) still shows grout and 14 in cast-in bolts. Those were superseded by Rev. 0A. Its apron and trench layout still apply. The drum has been replaced by the buried tank. The same sheet calls for an 8'-6" trench on an 8'-0" pad, so the trench overhangs each side by 3 in. Confirm that detail with the PE.

Open before it can be sealed

Rev. 0A §13
  1. Baseplate thickness and hole size for HDA-TR (holes must be enlarged to 22–23 mm).
  2. ASCE 7-22 wind at rest, plus an operational wind limit.
  3. Geotech: allowable bearing, subgrade preparation over the Lake Clifton fill, and μ = 0.40.
  4. Baseplate bending, welds and prying, now that the plate bears directly on concrete.
  5. The fabrication drawing still shows a 6 in pad. Change it to 15 in.
  6. Flame deflector geometry and anchorage. Keep the trench at least 6 ft from the deflector exit, since the polymer channel is rated to 180 °F.
  7. Engineer-of-record acceptance of HDA under fatigue and shock, which ESR-1546 §5.10 does not cover.
  8. F2 mix design submittal.
  9. PE seal, with GPR or cover-meter scan before drilling.
  10. Tank size. 300 gal is a placeholder. It needs to cover the RP-1 inventory + 10 % and a storm event.
  11. Tank buoyancy. An empty buried tank on the Lake Clifton fill can float in high groundwater, so it needs a hold-down slab or deadmen sized by the PE.
  12. Tank type and leak detection. Use a double-wall tank listed for flammable liquids (steel UL 58/1746 or fiberglass UL 1316), with interstitial monitoring, bonding and grounding.
  13. Permitting. A buried tank that collects hydrocarbons may be regulated as an underground storage tank. Confirm with Morgan State EHS and MDE before buying it.
This is a working design for review and pricing. A licensed PE has to review and stamp it under PWS-LC-FND-001, with special inspection during construction, before any test firing.
