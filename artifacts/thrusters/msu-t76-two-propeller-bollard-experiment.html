MSU-T76 Two-Propeller Bollard Experiment — Test Design

2026-11-05 · @u_MVqo1upVhTwZo_p9eVhfmw

Objective and hypotheses

The test measures whether two propellers printed from the same CAD file differ in bollard thrust and torque, and whether the validated BEMT model predicted that difference before the test. Prop A is SLA resin (50 µm layers); Prop B is FDM PLA (0.20 mm layers, printed flat). Everything else (duct, motor, rig, water) is shared.

  • H1 (torque): at equal rpm, torque coefficient KQ of B exceeds A by +16 to +35 % (median +24 %).

  • H2 (thrust): at equal rpm, thrust coefficient KT of A and B is equivalent within ±3 %.

  • H3 (fixed throttle): at equal ESC throttle, B produces 7–12 % less thrust and runs 4–5 % slower.

  • Model check: measured thrust and torque fall within ±10 % of the BEMT prediction for both propellers.

The hypotheses and predictions are fixed before testing, so the comparison with the model is a genuine blind check.

Test articles

Both propellers come from one STL (propeller.stl: Ka-type 4-70, D = 76 mm, P/D = 1.0, hub 0.22D) and run in the same duct on the same motor, so the print process is the only intended difference.

Shared hardware: one 19A-type duct (PETG, sanded and epoxy-coated, ks ≈ 5 µm), tip gap 0.75 mm, SunnySky X2216 880Kv motor, M5 collet adapter.

Inspect every copy before testing and record the values in the workbook:

  1. Mass (0.01 g) and tip diameter at 4 blades (caliper).

  2. Blade thickness and chord at 0.7R on all 4 blades.

  3. Pitch at 0.7R (height difference of LE and TE on a surface plate).

  4. Surface roughness Ra, Rq, Rsk on suction and pressure sides at 0.5R and 0.7R (profilometer, cut-off 0.8 mm, measured chordwise).

SLA and FDM also differ in geometric accuracy. The measured geometry lets you re-run the BEMT with each copy's real thickness and pitch, which separates the roughness effect from the geometry effect.

Rig and instrumentation

A two-axis gimbal measures thrust and torque mechanically on separate load cells, so torque does not depend on a motor model. Both axes intersect 700 mm above the tank floor; the thruster hangs below on a 2020 strut.

Measured quantities and derived coefficients

Each set-point gives the mean of 9 s of 10 Hz data (100 samples after a 1 s discard) for rpm, thrust T, torque Q, bus voltage, bus current and water temperature. Water density ρ and viscosity come from the measured temperature. The derived coefficients are:

K_T = \frac{T}{\rho n^2 D^4}, \qquad K_Q = \frac{Q}{\rho n^2 D^5}, \qquad \mathrm{FOM} = \frac{T^{3/2} / \sqrt{4 \rho A}}{2 \pi n Q}, \qquad A = \frac{\pi D^2}{4}

n is in rev/s and D = 0.076 m. FOM compares shaft power with the ideal power of a ducted actuator disk; a rougher blade lowers FOM because it raises Q at the same T. Overall efficiency uses electrical power V·I instead of shaft power.

Calibration

Calibrate in situ, with the thruster submerged, before Block 1 and again after Block 3; accept R² ≥ 0.999 and cross-talk below 1 % of full scale. Enter readings in the workbook's Calibration sheet, which computes the factors for calibration.json.

  1. Thrust (kA): tie braided line to the motor-mount ring at the thruster axis height. Run it horizontally upstream (−x, the thrust direction) over a pulley clamped to the back wall at axis height, then up over a second pulley at the rim. Hang 0, 200, 500, 1000, 1500, 2000 g, loading then unloading, 20 s per step.

  2. Torque (kB): clamp a horizontal bar to the strut above the water, perpendicular to the thrust axis. Hang 50–250 g at 60 mm on one side, then on the other side, to check symmetry.

  3. Cross-talk: record cell B during the thrust calibration and cell A during the torque calibration.

  4. RPM check: compare the hall-sensor rpm with a handheld optical tachometer at 1500 and 2000 rpm (in air, propeller removed, reflective tape on the bell).

  5. Drift: if pre- and post-test factors differ by more than 1 %, interpolate the factor linearly in time; otherwise use their mean.

In-situ calibration absorbs the lever ratio, bearing friction and buoyancy, so these do not enter the uncertainty budget separately.

Run procedure

Each run is one automated sweep of five set-points (about 2 min of motor time, about 25 min including the propeller swap and settling).

  1. Fill the tank the day before so dissolved air escapes; skim surface bubbles. Record water temperature.

  2. Mount the propeller listed in the Test matrix. Tighten the collet nut; confirm the tip gap by passing a 0.5 mm shim around the duct with no contact.

  3. Lower the thruster, tilt it once to release trapped air, and wait 2 min for still water.

  4. Run the command line from the Test matrix, e.g. python log_run.py --port COM5 --run 1 --prop B --copy 2 --repeat 1 --mode rpm. The script stops the motor, zeroes both cells, records 6 s of tare, then for each set-point 6 s settling plus 10 s recording, then a spin-down and an 8 s post-tare.

  5. Wait at least 3 min before the next run so the tank calms; log the end temperature and the zero drift the script reports.

  6. Fixed-throttle runs use --mode throttle (1150–1350 µs) with the same sequence.

  7. After each block, inspect blades for damage; at the end of the day rinse and dry the motor.

Repeat a run if any of these occur: zero drift above 0.1 N or 0.002 N·m, rpm standard deviation above 2 % in a record window, a surface vortex or air drawn into the duct, or a motor too hot to hold.

Test matrix, randomization and statistics

The design is 2 propellers × 2 printed copies × 3 repeats = 12 rpm-controlled sweeps (60 set-points), plus 4 fixed-throttle sweeps, run in 3 randomized blocks. The full run order with ready-to-paste command lines is in the workbook's Test matrix sheet (seed 790).

Analysis (implemented in analyze_experiment.py):

  • KQ, KT and FOM ~ propeller + rpm + propeller × rpm, OLS on copy means, F-tests.

  • At each rpm: Welch t-test of B vs A with Hedges' g.

  • H2 by TOST equivalence of KT with a ±3 % margin.

  • H3 from the fixed-throttle sweeps: thrust and rpm of B relative to A.

Sample size: detecting the conservative +15 % torque effect at α = 0.05 with power 0.9 needs 4 runs per propeller if run-to-run variation is 5 %, or 8 if it is 8 %. The plan has 6 per propeller. If the two copies of one propeller differ by more than 5 % in KQ, print and test a third copy.

Uncertainty budget

The expected standard uncertainty at 2000 rpm is about 1.2 % on KT and 1.5 % on KQ, well below the predicted 24 % torque difference. These are pre-test estimates (1σ); replace them with the values from your calibration and repeat runs.

At fixed motor voltage of 2.0 to 4.5 V (X2216 model), B is predicted to run 4–5 % slower and give 7–12 % less thrust. The bands come from a Monte-Carlo over the roughness-model inputs: measured-to-ideal Ra ratio, ridge-direction factor, support-scar factor, roughness function (Nikuradse or Colebrook) and transition threshold.

Model basis: the duct-propeller BEMT was checked against measured open-water data for a 60 mm Ka4-55 propeller in a JD7704 duct (He et al., 2015). With three duct parameters fitted on thrust only (σ = 1.13, close to the 1.10 geometric exit-area ratio), torque was predicted blind within 4 % for J ≤ 0.4.

Data analysis and comparison with simulation

One script turns the raw logs into the statistics, the model comparison and the paper figure; it was tested end to end on synthetic logs (folder demo_SYNTHETIC, never to be used as results).

  1. Copy the run logs into experiment/data/raw/.

  2. Put kA and kB from the Calibration sheet into calibration.json.

  3. Run python analyze_experiment.py. It applies the tare and linear drift correction, averages each record window, computes KT, KQ and FOM with temperature-corrected water properties, and writes steps.csv, copy_means.csv, stats.txt, comparison.csv and measured_vs_predicted.png.

  4. Update the model with what you measured: set the measured Ra of each propeller in the roughness model (replacing the assumed 0.5 measured-to-ideal ratio), re-run cases.py and two_prop_prediction.py, and report both the blind and the updated prediction.

  5. Decide the hypotheses from stats.txt: H1 by the propeller F-test and the per-rpm t-tests, H2 by TOST, H3 by the fixed-throttle ratios. The model passes if the mean absolute error is within ±10 % for thrust and torque.

The orientation experiment (printing on edge) is the natural follow-up: it identifies the ridge-direction factor that the model currently treats as an assumption (0.3–0.8).

Safety

The main hazards are mains power near 320 kg of water and a spinning propeller, and each has a fixed control.

  • Mains power only through a GFCI adapter; the 12 V supply sits on the floor at least 1 m from the tank.

  • 30 A fuse and a latching emergency-stop switch between the supply and the ESC; disarm (stop command, then switch off) before touching the thruster.

  • Never lower or lift the thruster with the ESC powered.

  • The filled tank weighs about 340 kg (water plus tank) on 53 × 31 in: place it on a ground-floor slab and confirm with the lab manager.

  • Wear safety glasses while the motor runs; keep loose cables tied to the frame, away from the duct inlet.

  • Wipe up spills immediately; keep the electronics box closed while testing.

Schedule and file index

The experiment fits in four weeks, with testing done in two half-days.

  1. Week 1: order parts (workbook BOM tab), print the rig parts, duct and the four propellers.

  2. Week 2: assemble the frame and gimbal, wire and flash the logger, tune the rpm loop, run calibration.

  3. Week 3: inspect geometry and roughness of all copies, run Blocks 1–3, repeat calibration.

  4. Week 4: run the analysis, update the BEMT with measured roughness, draft the results section.
